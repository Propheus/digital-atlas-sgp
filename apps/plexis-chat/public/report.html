01The keystone idea

One design decision shapes everything below.

Python computes every ground-truth answer deterministically from the atlas parquet; the model only learns to PHRASE and REASON — never to memorise a number.

Because correctness is computed, not trusted, the same engine plays four roles — and that is what makes a small budget produce a reliable model:

  • Data generator — emits exhaustive, correct Q&A across every area and metric.
  • Quality verifier — recomputes and rejects any pair whose numbers don't trace to the source.
  • Reward oracle — the verifier doubles as a verifiable RL reward (planned for v1 / GRPO).
  • Live tool source — the same functions serve fresh, exact facts at inference (RAG grounding).

Consequence: we could phrase the bulk with a cheap model and spend the expensive model only where reasoning is hard — total data cost ~$25 instead of ~$150.

Distinctness was enforced (small families enumerated, large pair-families de-duplicated with seen-sets) so the model learns the pattern, not a template.

Data-honesty rules baked in at generation

  • Normalise concentration — elder share, not raw count (else you just rank by population).
  • Percentile semantics — “#194 of 214 = bottom 9%”, never “top 91%”.
  • Structural zeros — HDB resale 0 = a no-HDB private enclave, not “cheap”.
  • Label honesty — hawker eateries (POIs) ≠ gazetted hawker centres.
  • OD = weekday monthly aggregate; no weekend / time-of-day data (a stated gap, not a guess).

So what did we actually fine-tune on?

Every training pair's answer is computed from real atlas facts — so the data is full of facts. But the model is not meant to memorise them. It learns the behaviour shown over the facts. The split:

Learned into the weights
  • How to reason over facts (compare, rank, aggregate, read OD)
  • Singapore-calibrated interpretation of a number
  • Data-semantics & honest abstention
  • Verdict-first voice & register
  • A thin layer of stable geography (region, concepts)
Stays in the atlas (RAG at inference)
  • The 174,713 place stats & per-area numbers
  • Populations, counts, walkability scores, distances
  • The full OD matrix
  • Anything that changes when the data refreshes
  • → injected into the prompt at query time, not baked in

A 12B can't hold 174K facts — and we didn't want it to. Ask “is Bedok North good for families?” and the numbers (86% HDB, 39 preschools) come from RAG; the reasoning, interpretation, abstention and voice come from the fine-tune.

Real training pairs (verbatim from the corpus)

Reasoning · OD asymmetry

Prompt → Context: 8,636 weekday trips Bedok North→Kampong Bugis, 9,210 the reverse. Which direction has more, and by what factor?

Trained answer → 9,210 > 8,636 → Kampong Bugis→Bedok North dominates; 9,210 / 8,636 ≈ 1.1×.

Learns: read an OD matrix, pick the larger, compute the ratio — not these specific trip counts.

Planning · analog estimate (deep reasoning)

Prompt → Teban Gardens (~16,759 residents) sits ~963 m from rail — under-served. By analogy with rail-served Fajar (walkability 0.69 vs Teban's 0.35)… what would a station do?

Trained answer → Direction well-grounded (accessibility & commercial activity likely rise), but magnitude uncertain — “an estimate, not a forecast.”

Learns: reason by analogy, commit to a direction, and preserve the caveat — never fabricate a magnitude.

Abstention

Prompt → How many golf courses are in Clementi Woods according to the atlas?

Trained answer → “The atlas does not track golf courses, so I cannot provide a count.”

Learns: recognise an untracked category and refuse — the discipline base models lack.

Fact-grounded (reason-in-context)

Prompt → Context: Serangoon Central has 88 beauty/salon services. How many beauty/salon services are there?

Trained answer → 88 — lifted from the provided context, not from memory.

Learns: trust and extract the provided fact rather than guess — the core RAG behaviour.

03Enhanced reasoning — and why it's better

The goal was never a memorised database. It was a reasoner that stays honest: skill in the weights, facts from the atlas. Here is why the reasoning is genuinely stronger than an off-the-shelf model.

  1. Reason-in-context is the trained default. ~70% of the corpus puts the atlas facts in the prompt and asks the model to extract, compare, aggregate and read OD flows over them — the exact production pattern, scored 88% on unseen areas.
  2. Multi-step chains, distilled and verified. 15K deep traces came from a frontier model, but only chains whose final answer matched the deterministic gold were kept (rejection sampling, ~81% accept). The model learns reasoning that is known to be correct, not merely fluent.
  3. It covers reasoning shapes most models fumble. OD asymmetry, multi-hop aggregation, arithmetic over entities, two-condition synthesis, and cosine-similarity analogy were each their own family — breadth a generic model has never been drilled on.
  4. Singapore-calibrated judgment. It knows what the numbers mean locally — that a walkability of 0.45 is mediocre on a 0–1 scale, that a high HDB share signals a heartland, that 15% self-containment means residents commute out. A raw model recites; this one interprets.
  5. Data-semantics awareness. It distinguishes hawker centres from hawker eateries, and knows commuter flows are weekday-monthly — quirks a generic model conflates and gets quietly wrong.
  6. Honest abstention — the discipline prompt-engineering can't buy. Out-of-scope questions (crime, weather, future prices) are refused cleanly. In testing, the base model ignored an explicit instruction to decline and described the area instead; the fine-tune refuses and answers the actual question asked.
  7. It answers the question, not “the area”. Trained to lead with a verdict and justify it, rather than emit a generic description — so “is X good for families?” returns a reasoned yes/no with the trade-offs, not a brochure.

The structural truth this validated: reasoning lives in the weights; facts come from the atlas. With context it reasons at ~88% and does not hallucinate; closed-book it refuses to invent — and the tool layer fills the gap.

04Methodology

Generation, then a lean supervised fine-tune on a single rented GPU.

Generation

  • Ground-truth from the Plexis v4.9.0 parquet (Python).
  • Phrasing by a cheap flash model; deep traces by a frontier model.
  • Numeric-fidelity QC + dedup; entity-holdout split (whole areas held out).
  • ~220K raw → ~160K curated; formulaic giants down-sampled for balance.

Known limits v1 targets

  • filter-then-rank from scratch (filt_super) — the hardest multi-step kind; the clearest reasoning gap.
  • precise closed-book recall — a 12B can't memorise 300+ areas × N stats; that's the tool layer's job.
  • over-abstention without context — over-anchored to the coverage list; tools make it moot.

An honest finding: the writing trade-off

Fine-tuning on a largely terse, factual corpus dulled the base model's natural eloquence (an “alignment tax”). But the trade runs both ways — and that is the real case for the fine-tune:

Alchemy (fine-tuned)

Disciplined: answers the actual question, refuses out-of-scope, never invents a figure. With a light “voice” instruction it also writes with character — “grandparents and toddlers sharing the void deck”.

Raw Gemma 12B + RAG

More flair out of the box — but undisciplined: asked for a crime rate (untracked), it ignored the question and described the area instead, even when explicitly told to decline.

Takeaway: prose can be recovered with a prompt; trustworthy abstention and calibration cannot — that is what the fine-tuning buys.

06Next steps

  1. Wire the atlas tools at inference. Closes the closed-book gap and returns exact, fresh figures — the single biggest lift, and the tool layer is already built.
  2. Fold in the 15K deep traces + GRPO. Reward = the deterministic verifier (a rare, free, verifiable signal) — directly targets filter-then-rank and other multi-step reasoning.
  3. Rebalance abstention. Fewer coverage-list anchors, more closed-book positives, so it's confident on in-scope casual questions.
  4. Retrain with richer, characterful answers. Keep the discipline, restore eloquence by default rather than by prompt — so flair and trustworthiness ship together.
  5. Productionise. Multi-turn, citations/provenance, a public URL, and a merged 16-bit serve.

One-liner: owning a deterministic atlas — generator, verifier, reward, and tool — let a small team build a grounded Singapore reasoning model that scores ~88% on unseen areas, abstains honestly, and is conservative rather than hallucinatory, for ~$25 of data plus a GPU rental.
