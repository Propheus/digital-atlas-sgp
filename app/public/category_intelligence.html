True Insights — What the Data Reveals
1
The government builds the scaffolding — the market fills it in
The strongest predictor for private F&B is SFA food licensing (55% for Fast Food, 39% for Restaurants). For private education, it's government preschools (39%). For convenience stores and beauty salons, it's CHAS clinics (36% and 22%). The pattern is unmistakable: government infrastructure doesn't just correlate with commercial activity — it creates the conditions for it. Where the government places a hawker centre, private restaurants follow. Where it builds preschools, tuition centres emerge. Public services generate the foot traffic that private commerce needs.
SFA → Fast Food R²=0.66 Preschools → Education R²=0.63 CHAS → Convenience R²=0.88
2
Bar districts and nightlife emerge from culture, not zoning
Bars are the 6th hardest category to predict (R²=0.44). No structural feature — not commercial zoning, not population density, not transit access — explains where bars cluster. Clarke Quay, Boat Quay, Ann Siang Hill became bar districts through cultural momentum and reputation, not because zoning maps said they should. The model's top predictor is just "total places" (36%) — a non-explanation. Similarly, culture & entertainment venues (R²=0.42) follow open space and road networks, not any demand signal. These are taste-driven categories that data infrastructure cannot predict.
Bar R²=0.44 Culture R²=0.42 No zoning signal found
3
Shopping is the most boring prediction — and that's the insight
83% of shopping prediction comes from a single feature: total place count. Where there are many places, there are many shops. This isn't a finding — it's a tautology. But it reveals something important: retail in Singapore has no structural preference. It doesn't care about transit, income, age, or zoning. It goes wherever commercial density exists. This makes retail the hardest category to create gap opportunities for — if a zone has retail gaps, it probably has gaps in everything.
Shopping: Total Places = 83% R²=0.83
4
Gyms follow families, not fitness culture
The #1 predictor for fitness centres isn't distance to MRT, commercial zoning, or income. It's population (26%) and government preschools (25%). Gyms in Singapore are a family-life amenity, not a lifestyle destination. They cluster where young families live — the same zones with preschools, HDB 4-5 room flats, and high residential density. The "hip boutique gym" narrative exists in Orchard and Tanjong Pagar, but the data says most of Singapore's fitness market is driven by parents who want a gym near their kids' school.
Population = 26% Gov Preschools = 25% R²=0.74
5
Industrial zoning is a secret office market signal
Industrial land use percentage is the #2 predictor for offices (34%) and a top-5 driver for business services, automotive, and general commerce. Singapore's "industrial" zones are not factories — they're JTC one-north, Changi Business Park, Mapletree Industrial parks. The zoning label "industrial" actually means "affordable commercial space." If you're looking for office market gaps, look at industrial zones with low office counts — that's where the next co-working hub lands.
LU Industrial → Office = 34% LU Industrial → Business = 12% LU Industrial → Services = 10%
6
Hawker culture is self-reinforcing — proximity to hawkers breeds more hawkers
Distance to nearest hawker centre is the #3 driver for hawker stalls (9%), alongside CHAS clinics (23%) and total places (23%). This means hawker food concentrates near existing hawker infrastructure — a self-reinforcing loop. The government builds the hawker centre; private street food stalls cluster within walking distance; the area becomes a "food zone." HDB 5-room flats (6%) also matter — hawker culture is strongest in mature HDB heartland estates, not new developments.
Dist Hawker = 9% HDB 5-Rm = 6% R²=0.73
7
Hotels predict hotels — tourism is an enclave economy
Hospitality is the hardest commercial category to predict (R²=0.22). The best structural predictor is "hotels within 1km" (11%) — meaning the only thing that predicts a hotel is other hotels nearby. Tourism in Singapore operates as enclave economies: Marina Bay, Orchard, Sentosa, Bugis. No amount of population, transit, or zoning data predicts where tourists stay. Tourism demand comes from international markets, not local infrastructure. This is a fundamentally different economic logic from every other category.
Hospitality R²=0.22 Hotels nearby = 11% Self-referential only
8
Condo percentage is Singapore's stealth income proxy
Singapore doesn't publish subzone-level income data. But condominium percentage acts as a near-perfect proxy. It's a top-5 driver for residential services (16%), health (2.2%), and influences premium categories. Zones with >30% condos (Cairnhill, River Valley, Bukit Timah) have categorically different commercial DNA than 80% HDB zones (Yishun, Woodlands, Jurong West). Dwelling type doesn't just describe who lives there — it predicts what commerce is viable.
Condo % → Residential = 16% Proxy for income No direct income data exists

Methodology

  • Model: Gradient Boosted Trees (300 trees, depth 5, lr 0.05, subsample 0.8)
  • Validation: 10-fold cross-validation, 5× repeated for stability. R² on original scale.
  • Features: 56 structural features. Zero place-derived features (no ratings, brands, reviews) to prevent leakage.
  • Transform: Best of raw vs y^0.25 (4th root) per target. Reduces skew, helps small-count zones.
  • Stage 1: Structural → total places (R²=0.772). Stage 2: Structural + total → each category.
  • Gap bands: Percentile-based. Top/bottom 15% = high signal. Middle 40% = within model noise.
