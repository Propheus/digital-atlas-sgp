The Question

NTUC FairPrice is Singapore's cooperative supermarket — 351 outlets serving 4.2 million residents. As the nation's largest grocery retailer with a social mission, coverage gaps matter. Can every resident walk to a FairPrice in 10 minutes?

802,685 residents (19.3%) live more than 800 metres from any FairPrice outlet — roughly a 10-minute walk. For the elderly (4,531 affected), families with young children, and mobility-impaired residents, this means they either take a bus to shop, pay for delivery, or rely on more expensive alternatives like Cold Storage.
Good news: Singapore's overall supermarket coverage is excellent. 80% of populated hexes have some supermarket within 500m (Giant, Sheng Siong, Cold Storage, independents). The gap is specifically FairPrice — which matters because FairPrice is consistently the most affordable option and many elderly prefer it by habit and price.

The Honest Assessment

What's Working
Overall supermarket coverage is excellent. 80% of residents have a supermarket within 500m. Singapore has no true food deserts at the grocery level — independent minimarts fill even the smallest gaps.

FairPrice median distance is 620m — within the acceptable walking threshold for most adults. The 351-store network covers 131 of 332 subzones.
What Needs Attention
802,685 residents (19.3%) are beyond 800m from FairPrice. For elderly on fixed incomes who rely on FairPrice's cooperative pricing, this means paying more at Cold Storage or spending 30+ minutes on a bus trip.

Yunnan (Jurong West) is a standout gap — 60K residents, 176 HDB blocks, and the nearest FairPrice is 1.4km away. This is not a marginal case.

Methodology

Data: 174,713 places from Google + Overture (V2), filtered to 351 FairPrice and 2,154 supermarket/grocery locations. Distances computed via haversine from 2,404 populated H3-9 hex centroids (175m resolution) to nearest outlet. Population distributed dasymetrically from Census 2025 using building footprints.

Threshold: 800m walking distance (~10 minutes), consistent with LTA's "acceptable access" standard for public transport planning.

Limitations: Distance is straight-line (haversine), not walking path. Actual walking distance is 15-30% longer due to road geometry. FairPrice Online delivery coverage not considered — this analysis focuses on physical store access for walk-in shoppers.
