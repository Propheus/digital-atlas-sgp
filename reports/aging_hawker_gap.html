Aging-Hawker Gap

1. Why This Question Matters

Hawker centres are not restaurants — they are publicly-built food infrastructure, originally created to clean street hawking off the streets in the 1970s and to provide affordable cooked food for low-income households. NEA's stall-rental subsidy and the 1 SGD-2 SGD price points make them functionally a food-security amenity, especially for the elderly:

  • Lower-income elderly disproportionately depend on hawker meals (cooking-at-home barriers: small kitchens, mobility, single-person households).
  • Hawker centres double as a third place for elderly — loneliness mitigation, daily routine, social contact.
  • UNESCO recognized Singapore's hawker culture as Intangible Cultural Heritage in 2020. The infrastructure is policy.
Test: at planning-area resolution, where does the supply of hawker stalls fall short of what the elderly population would predict? The companion Hawker Centre 2.0 programme (NEA, 2011 onwards, 20+ new centres) was a direct policy response to perceived gaps. Has it closed them?

2. National Picture

Across Singapore: 765,020 residents aged 65+, served by 5,837 hawker stalls across 103 existing hawker centres. The national average is 7.6 stalls per 1,000 elderly. But the geographic distribution is wildly uneven.

3. The Headline Finding — Zero-Centre Towns

Seven planning areas have zero existing hawker centres within their boundary, yet each holds at least 13,000 residents aged 65+. Together they house 191,950 elderly — 25% of Singapore's total elderly population.

Pattern: every zero-centre town is post-1980s. Singapore's first-generation HDB towns (Toa Payoh, Ang Mo Kio, Bedok, Bukit Merah) were built around hawker centres. From the 1990s onward, the planning template shifted — new towns rely on kopitiams in HDB precincts and mall food courts for cooked-food supply, not standalone NEA hawker centres. That's not necessarily wrong, but it represents an infrastructural-substitution decision that this report has not yet measured.

6. The Real Question — Is "Zero Centres" Actually Underserved?

The headline number is dramatic, but it would be wrong to conclude that Punggol elderly are starving. The honest framing:

  • Hawker centres are one channel of cooked-food supply — not the only one. New towns substitute kopitiams (HDB precinct coffee shops), mall food courts (Kopitiam-brand chains, Food Republic), and 24-hour QSR. In the source data: Punggol has multiple food courts and ~20 kopitiams it would count under those categories.
  • Hawker centres have unique properties the substitutes lack: public ownership, rental subsidy, 2 SGD price points, multi-stall variety in one location, social-meeting-place function. The substitution is real but imperfect.
  • The policy question isn't "is anyone starving" — it's "are the affordability + community-third-place properties of hawker centres being replicated by their substitutes?" Probably not, but worth measuring.
Phase-2 work item: rerun this analysis with extended cooked-food supply: hawker stalls + kopitiam stalls + food-court stalls + coffee-shop seats, weighted by price tier. Then compare the gap. If the extended supply still fails in the new towns — that's a bigger story. If it closes the gap — that's evidence the substitution works.

7. Methodology — What's Done vs. Planned

Done in this v0.1 (local data)

  • SingStat 2025 demographics aggregated to PA: total + 65+ residents (pop_age_sex_tod_2025.csv, 100,929 rows).
  • NEA hawker-centres geojson (amenities/hawker_centres.geojson, 129 features) filtered to STATUS=Existing; cooked-food stalls summed.
  • Spatial join: each hawker centre → planning-area polygon (point-in-polygon, shapely).
  • Computed stalls / 1k elderly per PA. Filtered to PA elderly ≥ 5k for table; PA total population ≥ 10k for ranking.

Phase 1 — Validation (next; mostly local)

  • Validate the PA attribution for boundary cases (Tampines Round Market, Yishun Chong Pang — both showing as 0 or 1 here are likely under-counted).
  • Cross-check NEA centre count against the 26 proposed/under-construction centres — some "0" PAs may have a Hawker 2.0 site already in the pipeline.

Phase 2 — Extended cooked-food supply (atlas-1)

  • Pull kopitiam, coffee-shop, food-court, and HDB-precinct void-deck commercial counts per PA. The atlas already has 295 places coded as "Hawker Centre" (vs. NEA's 103) — the extras are likely kopitiams; needs reconciliation.
  • Recompute extended-supply gap metric.

Phase 3 — Walk accessibility (atlas-1)

  • For every HDB block (13,267 total), compute walk distance to nearest hawker centre and to nearest extended-cooked-food site, using the existing 214K-node OSM walk graph.
  • Headline metric: % of elderly population within 400m / 800m / 1.2km walk of a hawker centre, per town.
  • This is the metric that matters for elderly — absolute counts are the wrong unit. Mobility-impaired residents need nearby centres, not town-total centres.

Phase 4 — Future-state scenario simulation

  • Use the scenario_sim Huff-gravity model already in the atlas (/scenario_sim/engine) to test: what does adding 5 strategically-placed hawker centres in zero-centre towns do to elderly access?
  • Output: ranked candidate sites for the next round of NEA Hawker Centre 2.0 builds.
Gate before Phase 4: Phase-2 extended-supply analysis must complete first. If the gap closes once kopitiams are counted, the policy framing changes from "build more hawker centres" to "is kopitiam-dependent food access equivalent in affordability and community function?" — and Phase 4 should pivot accordingly.

8. Honest Limitations

  • Hawker centres ≠ all cooked-food supply. This v0.1 measures one specific NEA-managed asset class. Kopitiams, food courts, and coffee shops are not counted. New towns lean heavily on these substitutes — not measuring them risks overstating the gap.
  • Planning-area attribution is point-in-polygon. Centres at PA borders may be assigned to the "wrong" town. Tampines & Yishun results are likely under-counted by 1-2 centres each.
  • "Stalls per elderly" assumes elderly are the relevant denominator. Hawker centres serve all ages. The metric is useful for surfacing access disparity, not absolute supply adequacy.
  • No walk-distance data here. A centre "in the town" may be a 25-minute walk from where most elderly actually live. Phase 3 fixes this.
  • Hawker Centre 2.0 pipeline (26 proposed/under-build) not yet allocated. Some zero-PAs may already have a NEA-pipeline centre — this v0.1 doesn't credit those. Phase 1 fixes.
