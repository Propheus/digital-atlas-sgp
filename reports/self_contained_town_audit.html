1. The Hypothesis

Singapore's HDB towns were designed under a self-containment doctrine: each new town should function as a complete settlement, with residents able to access daily needs — food, schooling, primary healthcare, transit, retail, recreation — without leaving the town. This was both a planning principle (HDB / URA) and a social policy (reduce CBD-bound commuting, build community at the precinct scale).

Test: for each of Singapore's ~23 HDB towns, measure whether the full reference amenity stack is present. Then go beyond presence/absence: measure per-capita adequacy, walk accessibility, and divergence from the planning ideal.

Why this is worth doing

  • Nobody (publicly) has run this with hex-resolution amenity data and current-year demographics. URA & HDB have it internally; published research is at MSA/town centroid resolution.
  • Singapore is small, planned, and dense — making it the cleanest natural experiment for the "complete town" hypothesis vs. real-world urban dynamics.
  • Findings are policy-relevant: which towns are "hollowing out" (mature estates losing amenities) and which new towns are "underbuilt" (BTO completions outpacing amenity rollout).

4. The Real Story Isn't Presence — It's Adequacy

The headline finding is that 21 of 23 towns score 10/12 or above on raw presence. The doctrine, in the binary sense, holds. But the absolute counts in the table above show wild variation that the binary score hides:

  • Library access is thin everywhere. 9 of 23 towns have zero places coded as Library; the rest have 1 or 2. Either NLB town libraries are under-coded in the place taxonomy, or libraries genuinely sit at the precinct edge. Phase-1 verify against NLB official list.
  • Hawker access varies 7×. Bedok has 23 hawker centres; Choa Chu Kang and Sembawang have 3 each — for towns of comparable resident populations. This is the headline finding driving the companion Aging-Hawker Gap Report.
  • Sports facility variance is even wider. Kallang has 27 (driven by Singapore Sports Hub); Pasir Ris and Ang Mo Kio have 3 each. The "town stadium" ideal lives unevenly.
  • Mature central estates over-index on amenity density. Geylang, Bukit Merah, Kallang, Bedok dominate every column. New towns (Punggol, Sengkang, Sembawang) under-index on hawker, GP, and supermarket counts despite full presence.
Implication: the LKY-era doctrine of "complete towns" survives as a checklist but is being eroded as per-capita adequacy. New towns hit the checklist; mature central estates hold the depth. This is exactly the kind of structural divergence worth quantifying at hex9 resolution.

5. Methodology — What's Done vs. What's Planned

Done in this v0.1

  • Joined data/places/sgp_places.jsonl (66,851 places with planning-area attribution) to HDB town list.
  • Counted matching place_type per town for each of the 12 reference amenities.
  • Computed binary stack-completeness score per town.
  • Pulled SingStat 2025 demographics (data/demographics/pop_age_sex_tod_2025.csv, 100,929 rows) for population context.

Phase 1 — Adequacy normalization (next)

  • Per-1k-resident rates for each amenity, per town, with confidence bands.
  • Resolve attribution issues (Yishun MRT, library under-coding, hawker boundary cases).
  • Validate library count against NLB official town-library list.
  • Validate MRT count against LTA station list (231 stations + 44 LRT).

Phase 2 — Walk-accessibility (atlas-1 server)

  • Use the existing 214K-node OSM walk graph (already on atlas-1) to compute, for every HDB block, walk distance to the nearest instance of each of the 12 amenities.
  • Headline metric: % of population within 10-min / 15-min walk of each amenity, per town.
  • Server task — local CSV-only analysis cannot reach the walk graph.

Phase 3 — Divergence from planning ideal

  • Build a "town signature" per HDB town using super_128d embeddings aggregated over the town's hex9 cells.
  • Cluster the 23 towns; do mature estates form a distinct cluster from new towns?
  • For each town, compute distance from cluster centroid — outliers are the towns that diverge most from their peer group.

Phase 4 — Maturity gradient

  • Combine HDB block year_completed (range: 1937 — 2025) with current amenity stock.
  • Fit typical amenity build-out curve over 20 years post-completion.
  • Identify outliers: towns whose stack lags or leads expectations given their build age.
Gate before Phase 2: all attribution caveats above must be resolved. If Yishun MRT is genuinely missing in source data (vs. an attribution bug), that's a much bigger data-quality issue than this report can address.

6. Honest Limitations

  • Place taxonomy is the bottleneck. Library and Community Club categories appear under-populated in the current snapshot. NLB has ~28 town libraries; we count 20 places coded as Library across all of Singapore.
  • Planning-area attribution is point-in-polygon. Amenities at PA boundaries (especially MRT stations near borders) may be assigned to the wrong town. Yishun MRT being 0 is the clearest symptom.
  • Binary presence vs. depth. A town with 1 GP clinic for 200K residents passes the same "stack score" as a town with 39 GPs for 200K residents. Phase 1 fixes this.
  • Hawker-centre count alone undercounts food access in new towns, which substitute kopitiams + mall food courts for the traditional hawker centre. The companion Aging-Hawker Gap Report addresses this directly.
  • No walk-distance data here. A library "in the town" might be a 25-minute walk from most residents. Phase 2 fixes this on the server.

  • v1.0 — adequacy-normalized scorecard + walk-accessibility metrics (requires atlas-1).
