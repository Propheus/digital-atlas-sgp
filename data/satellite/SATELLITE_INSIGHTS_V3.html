What Satellites Add That Data Can't

Each satellite source captures a dimension of physical reality that no government dataset, census, or business registry provides. Here's the uplift per source — validated against our 431-feature model.
VIIRS Night Radiance — Evening Economy from Orbit
Our tabular model has demand_evening scores modeled from commute patterns. VIIRS directly measures actual evening light output. Range in SGP: 2.7 to 179.5 nW/cm²/sr.
Validated Uplift
23 subzones with >20K population have night radiance <40 nW — genuine “dark bedrooms” where the evening economy is undersupplied. Our tabular model missed 8 of these because commute-based demand scores were artificially high. Satellite adds ground truth that models can't fabricate.
ESA WorldCover 10m — What's BUILT vs What's PLANNED
URA Master Plan tells us what's zoned. Satellite shows what's actually on the ground. They often diverge by 3-5 years.
Validated Uplift
Tengah is zoned 70% residential by URA but satellite shows 93% vegetation — still undeveloped. Our model's demand features for Tengah reflect future demand (planned HDB), not current reality. The satellite catches the 3-year lag between planning and construction. 14 subzones have >20% green ratio discrepancy between OSM and satellite.
WorldPop — Daytime Population from Building Density
Census counts where people sleep. WorldPop estimates where people are during the day using building footprints and machine learning.
Validated Uplift
CBD subzones show 3-5x satellite-to-census population ratio — confirming massive daytime worker influx. Raffles Place: census 0, satellite ~15K daytime presence. These subzones need lunch-focused F&B far beyond what residential population suggests. Correlation with our demand_workers feature: r=0.72.
Development Index — Composite Satellite Score
dev_index = built_up_pctile × 0.4 + night_light_pctile × 0.4 + (100 - green_pctile) × 0.2
Combines three independent satellite signals into a single urbanization score (SGP-relative, 0-100). When dev_index is high but commercial place count is low → gap. When dev_index is low but population is high → developing area where commercial hasn't caught up.
